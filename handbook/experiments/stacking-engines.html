What changes when the same subs go through four stackers on defaults?

Across four of five single-night targets and the multi-night M 51 test, the engines produced similar signal-normalized noise at the measured 2–4 pixel scale. Frame selection and registration failures produced larger differences, while StackingWizard consistently produced wider stars.

Evidence section: Results: blind rankings (preferences)

Protocol and scoring

Round A: NOVA’s eccentricity gate, then each tool’s own default selection. Round A is not an engine comparison.

Round B: identical production-culled frames, with tool culling off where possible (StackingWizard “tick all”).

Every master is plate-solved and its luminance reprojected with nearest-neighbour interpolation onto the Siril master’s pixel grid. Common coverage is the fraction of that full grid where all four engines have finite, nonzero pixels, after a 20-pixel binary erosion. Noise uses only sky within that intersection, excluding star masks dilated by 4 pixels and bright structure. Flux is matched on signal-dominated pixels, with Siril as the reference scale.

Band-limited noise is measured at 0–1, 1–2, 2–4, 4–8 and 8–16 px. Star FWHM, eccentricity and count use one detector. The reported 2–4 px value is signal-normalized noise, not proof of equal depth. A depth interpretation requires comparable effective resolution; 0–1 px is especially sensitive to interpolation character. Never rank engines by per-pixel SNR.

Split halves: odd/even frames by capture time, all four engines on each half, 40 masters.

Split-half outcomes

M 45, M 101 and M 51 gaps of 1–3.6 % reproduce, with H1/H2 spread ≤ 0.7 %.

M 1 and NGC 281 gaps under about 1.5 % are unresolved.

StackingWizard’s wider stars reproduce on all five targets in both halves (+0.11 to +0.28 px, spread ≤ 0.05 px).

On M 51, WBPP’s full-B gap (−3.6 %) lies outside its half range (−2.6 / −2.3 %); these are half-stack results; not confidence intervals or bounds on the full-stack result.

Evidence section: Results: round B (same frames)

NGC 281 — registration failure

SyQon accepted 351 of 1,015 frames. Its log reports “664 frame(s) excluded: Both intensity and stellar registration failed”. This is a registration failure, not stacking quality, and explains the low common coverage (0.456).

NGC 281 had substantially greater pointing spread. Spread alone establishes neither rotation nor the cause of the alignment failure.

Evidence section: Claim checks

Round A — not an engine comparison

StackingWizard is the only engine that rejects whole subs by default. Selection differs here; these are not pure engine differences.

The gated input was 1,373 for M 1 and 1,165 for NGC 281. Their “gated in” table entries are Siril’s stacked counts; SW removals use the gated input. The √N prediction is √(Siril subs ÷ SW subs) − 1.

On the same gated inputs across these five targets, StackingWizard’s own frame selection changed its median star width by −0.05 to +0.01 px, which is negligible. Its signal-normalized 2–4 px noise was 2–20 % higher than when it kept every frame. Its wider stars than Siril’s therefore come from its stacking, not its selection.

Selection, weighting and correlations prevent attributing the smaller-than-baseline noise penalty to weighting.

Evidence section: Results: finished-image showcase (not evidence)

Culling, and the Seestar’s own stack

A follow-up on the same five nights. For each target, Siril stacked every sub from the night. Two culled Siril stacks were compared with it: Round A drops only subs with elongated stars, and Round B is the full cull. The Seestar’s own live stack from that night was compared too.

In these five nights, the culled Siril restacks had higher 2–4 px noise than restacking all retained night frames. The Round-B cuts also had lower measured median star widths in all five cases, by about .03–.14 px. That is a trade, not a free win. The noise penalty was smaller than a simple equal-quality, independent-frame count model predicts; that model does not identify the cause.

The Seestar’s same-night live outputs had broader measured stars and lower fine-scale noise. At 4–16 px, the measured noise levels were within about 3 % of Siril’s. That is a noise comparison, not proof of equal depth. M 51 is not an identical-frame comparison: 1,113 stacked live versus 1,142 retained subs restacked.

Because effective sharpness differs, we do not interpret lower 2–4 px noise as extra depth here. Smoothing or resampling could contribute; these results do not identify the Seestar’s internal method.

Finished images, ranked blind — preferences

All four versions of each target — all subs, the roundness check, the full cull and the Seestar’s own stack — went through NOVA’s full pipeline. Jeff ranked 200 px crops blind, with the letters reshuffled per crop. The key opened only after all five targets were ranked.

No version stood out: across 15 crops each averaged between 1.8 and 2.0. On M 1, where all four versions got the same processing choices, Jeff put the Seestar’s stack second in every crop. Three of the Seestar’s five outright wins came where the pipeline had processed it differently (a different stretch on M 45, a different colour path on NGC 281), so those may reflect processing more than the stack.

The first M 1 runs used an older pipeline whose stretch picker left the Crab too dim; M 1 was rerun on the current pipeline (workflow 2.14.2) before ranking. These are preferences on finished images, never stack-quality evidence.

Side results

Laptop vs VM Siril, M 51 Round B (1,041 subs): Siril 1.4.4 for Windows ran NOVA’s script. 0–1 px noise was 1.615 both; FWHM 2.81 vs 2.80; eccentricity 0.378 vs 0.380; stars 503 vs 505; median relative |Δ| 4.5e-5. Agreement is about one part in twenty thousand.

M 1’s weak finished images on 3 of 4 engines came from NOVA’s stretch-picker bug, not the stacks. The grain cap collapsed on a degenerate candidate; Siril’s exactly-zero grain skipped the cap. The bug is fixed.

Pilot, not cohort: an early M 52 test used 20 subs. SW reported SNR 33 vs WBPP 26, with neighbour correlation 0.50 vs 0.27 and equal band-limited noise. This motivated the rule against ranking by per-pixel SNR.

Evidence section: Side results

What we cannot claim

  • Any engine is "best" in general: there are five targets, one night each, and default settings.
  • Anything about mosaics or drizzle (experiments 2 and 3).
  • Anything about speed beyond "indicative on this VM".
  • That SW's rejection is always worse. On M 45 its measured 2–4 px noise was similar to Siril’s.
  • Any NOVA grade as a measure of stack quality.

Reproduce it

  • Use the scripts/stackexp/ harness on main: stage.py, verify_inputs.py, run_target.sh, run_finale.sh and score_target.py. Engine wrappers record runs; score.py implements common-grid scoring.
  • Archive references are descriptive, not public download links. Request the experiment archive and original subs from Jeff Henry to rerun; this page does not distribute input data or licensed engines.
  • Per-target inventories: stackexp-<target>/inventory.json; Round-B scores: B/score_v2/scores.json. M 51 uses stackexp-m51n and stackexp-m51m; the finale uses stackexp-m51all-r2/manifest.json (identity 4eaa24a6) and scores.json.
  • Frozen scorer archive: stackexp-archive-20261025/score.py (common coverage and sky masks checked against this archived scorer). Split halves: stackexp-lanes/halves_summary.json. Blind rankings: stackexp-lanes/lane2_v3/henry_blind_rankings_v3b{,_repeat}.json. Showcase choices: stackexp-lanes/heroes/hero_picks.json.
  • These datasets support this version-specific comparison, not a general law. Mosaics are a later experiment.
